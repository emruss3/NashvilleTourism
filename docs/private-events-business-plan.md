# Nashville.com Private Events Marketplace — Business Plan

Prepared for BPH Nashville.com, LLC · September 28, 2026 · Companion to `private-events-marketplace-build-brief.md`. Background only: where this plan and the brief differ, the brief wins.

## 1. Summary

Nashville.com will operate a Nashville-only referral marketplace for private events. Planners browse venues with real capacities and pricing, send one brief to up to five venues, and venues reply within 24 business hours. Nashville.com is paid a **5% success fee by the venue** only when an event books. We hold no payments, sign no contracts, and run no events.

The wedge is data and speed in one city, which national platforms cannot afford. The moat is the domain: nashville.com will own the search results for every "event venue Nashville" query within months of transfer, and every hotel, tour and event visitor on the site is a planner in waiting.

Target at month 12: 40+ listed venues, 150 briefs/month, 20 booked events/month, ~$12K/month in fees, growing with instant-book packages and sponsored placement. It is a modest line on its own; its strategic value is that it converts nashville.com's group traffic into contracted, attributable revenue and gives BPH's own venues a demand channel on arm's-length terms.

## 2. The opportunity

**Group demand is large and structurally underserved online.**

- Nashville drew 17.4M visitors in 2025 (11.6M overnight); 17% of overnight visitors travel as groups and 19% on business, per Tourism Economics/NCVC. Hotel room nights sold reached 10.3M.
- Cvent ranks Nashville the #3 meeting destination in North America; two of Cvent's top five hotels nationally are in Nashville. Music City Center has 14 definite citywides between August and November 2026 alone and roughly 40 for 2027, each 1,000–4,800 peak rooms. Every citywide brings dozens of sponsor dinners, client receptions and team outings that book locally, often within weeks of the event.
- Bachelorette and celebration travel is Nashville's signature leisure segment and books almost entirely online, on short notice, in groups of 8–30.

**How planners find venues today** is a mix of the CVC (which serves the convention itself, not its vendors), national marketplaces with thin Nashville data, DMCs and planners at 10%+, and Google. The gap: a fast, accurate, local answer to "where can 60 of us go Tuesday night."

**Why Nashville.com wins:** domain authority for exactly these searches; existing visitor traffic already planning trips; the hotel and tour layers as lead sources; BPH Hospitality's three venues as anchor supply and a live test bed; and no need to make money on the marketplace alone, so we can price at 5% and win supply on terms.

## 3. Product (see build brief for specification)

1. **Listings with real numbers.** Every space publishes seated/standing capacity, a price field (room fee, minimum spend, per-person or buyout) and constraints. Nothing publishes without them.
2. **Multi-venue brief with an SLA.** One brief to up to five venues; 24-business-hour reply commitment tracked automatically; misses trigger reminders and, on a second miss, alternatives to the planner.
3. **Verified big-date availability.** For CMA Fest, Titans home games, NYE and citywides: open/limited/booked per venue.
4. **Instant-book packages.** Priced semi-private packages for 8–40 guests, deposit taken on the venue's own system.
5. **Venue dashboard.** Leads, reply, outcomes, listing editor, availability. Turns the marketplace from an email chain into a system of record.
6. **Trust.** Ownership disclosure on BPH venues, sponsored labeling, ranking independent of fees and ownership, no fake reviews.

## 4. Business model

| Stream | Terms | When |
|---|---|---|
| Success fee | 5% of contracted venue spend (rental + F&B minimum), $250 minimum, all venues incl. BPH-owned | Phase 1 |
| Instant-book packages | 5% of package price | Phase 3 |
| Sponsored placement | Monthly flat fee, max 3 slots per browse page, labeled | Phase 3 |
| Hotel blocks | Group room requests routed to the LiteAPI path; margin per the hotel line | Later |

Not offered: pay-per-lead, listing subscriptions, payment processing. Each would either degrade lead quality or put Nashville.com in the operations chain.

**Fee capture.** Venues self-report bookings in the dashboard; the referral agreement requires it and grants audit on request. Expect 15–25% leakage in year one; reduce it with the dashboard, quarterly reconciliation against inquiry lists, and the fact that repeat supply depends on staying listed.

**Payback.** At base case the line covers its one hire by month 10–12 and is contribution-positive thereafter. The low case does not cover the hire; the decision gate in §9 exists for that reason.

## 6. Go-to-market

**Supply (months 0–3).**

- Anchor: JBJ's, Hank's, Playdate, live on identical terms, first week.
- First ten partners: hand-picked rooftops, Gulch/12 South/Germantown restaurants with private rooms, two music venues, two event spaces. Chosen for data quality and responsiveness, not size.
- Each venue: site visit, photos, signed referral one-pager, listing built by us, sales contact trained on the reply link.
- Forty venues by month 6; 60–80 by month 12. Depth per venue over breadth.

**Demand.**

- **SEO** is the primary channel: hub, venue, occasion, neighborhood and size pages, all indexable with real numbers. Launch pages on nashroam.com now; they inherit nashville.com's authority at cutover.
- **Convention calendar.** For each citywide, a landing page ("Receptions near Music City Center, Oct 8–14") and outbound to exhibitor/sponsor lists, timed 6–10 weeks out.
- **On-site cross-sell.** Group-size searches on hotels (6+), bachelorette content, tours for 8+, and the events calendar all surface a "planning something for a group?" path.
- **Planners and DMCs.** Offer them the shortlist tool as a free workflow; they bring the biggest briefs and already expect venues to pay them, so our 5% sits beside theirs without conflict.

**Positioning line:** "Nashville's private events desk. Real capacities, real prices, replies in 24 hours."

## 7. Operations

- **Venue relations lead** owns supply, data quality, SLA compliance and fee reconciliation. This role is the business.
- **Events desk inbox** receives every brief; nothing depends on someone reading it, but someone does daily.
- **SLA process:** automatic reminders at 24h, alternatives to the planner at 48h, venue placement demoted after three misses in a quarter, delisted after six.
- **Reconciliation:** quarterly, per venue, against the inquiry list; invoices monthly on self-reported bookings.
- **Content cadence:** occasion and neighborhood intros reviewed quarterly; key-date calendar maintained 12 months out; big-date availability refreshed by venues 60 days before each date.

## 8. Governance and related parties

- Fees flow to BPH Nashville.com, LLC. Venue agreements are with Newco.
- BPH Hospitality venues sign the same referral agreement at the same 5% and pay it. This is disclosed on-site and in investor materials as a related-party arrangement on market terms; it turns house demand into Newco Cash Flow that counts toward the seller earnout and the Preferred waterfall.
- Ranking and placement rules are written into the codebase and unit-tested (ownership and fee never affect order). That test is the compliance control.

## 9. Milestones and decision gates

## 10. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Venues under-report bookings | Dashboard as system of record; reconciliation; delist for non-reporting; 5% is low enough that cheating isn't worth the relationship |
| Venues ignore leads | SLA automation, demotion, and the planner-side "alternatives" step keeps the promise even when a venue fails |
| Perceived self-dealing (BPH venues) | Identical terms, on-page disclosure, ranking test, investor disclosure |
| Thin data at launch | Publish check blocks incomplete listings; better 12 complete venues than 40 partial |
| CVC or national platform reaction | We serve a segment (vendor events, small groups) the CVC doesn't; national platforms can't match local depth |
| Demand never materialises | Month-6 gate; the build is reusable as a house channel and the SEO pages retain value |

## 11. What success looks like in 18 months

A planner searching for a venue in Nashville lands on nashville.com, sees ten spaces that fit with real prices, sends one brief, and has three replies by the next morning. Sixty venues treat the site as their best-converting lead source. BPH's three venues fill Sunday-to-Wednesday receptions they used to miss. And Newco books $150–250K a year in fees from a line that costs one salary.
