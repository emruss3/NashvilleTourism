# NSVL brand guide

## 1. Brand idea
**One good plan changes everything.**

NSVL connects people to Nashville through music, neighborhoods, shared experiences and things they want to wear. Nashville.com is the practical destination that turns curiosity into a plan. The apparel should carry belonging beyond the city; the website should earn repeat use from locals as well as visitors.

Brand promise: make it easy to find a worthwhile Nashville experience and bring a piece of the city with you.

Audience: culturally curious adults, roughly 25–45; locals making weekly plans, visitors organizing a trip, and apparel buyers who care about design. Design must also remain usable for broader ages and accessibility needs.

Character: confident, social, useful, independent, welcoming. Edge comes from the sharp existing mark, strong typography, decisive cropping and real culture—not visual clutter. Avoid generic tourism graphics, cowboy clichés, souvenir slogans and imitation sports-team monograms.

## 2. Naming and hierarchy
| Context | Name |
|---|---|
| Main visual identity / apparel | NSVL |
| Supporting logo descriptor | NASHVILLE |
| Domain / destination / attribution | Nashville.com |
| Spoken and editorial city name | Nashville |
| Store CTA | Shop NSVL |
| Search titles | Descriptive topic followed by Nashville.com |

NSVL and Nashville.com are one identity, not two separate businesses in the customer journey. Explain once in About/footer: “NSVL is Nashville.com—a guide to the city and the things that make it worth coming back.” Do not label the redesign “Black / Cream”; that is a concept-board label.

## 3. Logo system
Use the exact wide, connected NSVL mark from the selected image, including its distinctive angles and letter spacing. Preserve the NASHVILLE descriptor as a separate optional lockup element. Do not recreate it with typed letters, replace it with NV/NSH, skew, round, outline or stretch it.

Primary: Charcoal Ink logo on Paper White. Reverse: Paper White logo on Charcoal Ink. Use these two brand colors throughout the site and apparel. Black and white are neutral reproduction alternatives.

Clear space: at least one quarter of the mark's height on each side. Starting digital minimums: 88px wide for standalone NSVL and 140px for the descriptor lockup; verify the actual vector at those sizes. Below those sizes omit the descriptor. Do not cram the wide wordmark into a tiny favicon: request a separately approved small-size derivative. No new monogram is authorized by this brief.

Required production exports after vector approval: primary and reversed SVG; transparent PNG at 512, 1024 and 2048px; horizontal lockup; standalone mark; approved favicon SVG/ICO/PNG; social avatar; embroidery-ready single-color artwork. Preserve a source master outside the web export folder.

## 4. Color
| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Paper White | #F5F3ED | Primary page surface, light apparel |
| Charcoal Ink | #1F2421 | Text, logo, dark bands and primary buttons |
| Soft Paper | #EAE7DE | Secondary panels and form surfaces |
| Muted Ink | #59605A | Supporting text |
| Border | #D5D7CE | Decorative dividers; not the sole control boundary |

Use Paper White and Charcoal Ink across branded surfaces. Supporting neutral tones serve borders, muted text and secondary surfaces. No third brand accent color. Avoid brown/sepia grading and beige gradients.

Use Paper White text on Charcoal Ink for primary buttons. Links use contextual ink with underlines. Use clear labels, borders and icons for selected, error and availability states; never rely on color alone. Check contrast on each actual surface, including photography.

## 5. Typography
Proposed implementation pairing: Manrope for display and headings; Inter for body and interface. These are typesetting recommendations, not a reconstruction of the custom logo. Use locally hosted licensed WOFF2 files with font-display: swap; verify font licensing/source during implementation. If unavailable use system sans temporarily and record that substitution.

Display: 700–800 weight, tracking -0.045em, line-height 0.98–1.05. Desktop hero 64–88px; mobile 40–52px. H2 36–48px desktop, 28–34px mobile. H3 22–28px. Body 16–18px, line-height 1.5–1.65. Small labels 12px, letter-spacing 0.14em, uppercase sparingly. Buttons 15–16px, weight 600. Avoid light-weight gray body text.

Do not make all headings oversized. Hierarchy is hero, section heading, card title, metadata. Limit copy width to 55–70 characters. Use real text, not rasterized screenshot text.

## 6. Photography and art direction
Show people doing something together: intimate shows, independent restaurants, galleries, neighborhood streets, daytime patios, creative work. Mix people, places and product; keep the city identifiable through verified locations. Favor natural light, strong framing and believable social moments over forced stock-photo smiles. Avoid using only Broadway neon or generic skyline photos.

Hero: one strong horizontal image with clear negative space on the left and faces protected on the right. Provide a separate mobile crop, not just a narrow center crop. Use a restrained charcoal scrim for readable copy. Product photography: consistent paper or charcoal background, front/back/detail views, accurate fabric color and honest garment proportions.

Use original, licensed or explicitly approved photography. Generated mockups are art-direction references; do not present their street signs, venues, people or products as factual inventory. Track rights, source, alt text and focal point in the asset manifest.

## 7. Voice and copy
Write as a well-connected local who makes choosing easy. Short, specific and useful. Support recommendations with who, where, when and why. Avoid “ultimate,” “unforgettable,” “hidden gem” as a default, unsupported “best” claims and exaggerated exclusivity.

Approved lines:
- Hero: Get into Nashville.
- Hero support: Great music. Good food. Better company.
- Brand / trip-planning headline: One good plan changes everything.
- Neighborhood heading: Find your corner.
- Events heading: On the calendar.
- Merchandise heading: Good here. Good anywhere.
- Newsletter heading: Your next good plan.

CTAs should describe the result: Explore the city; Find events; Shop NSVL; Build a trip; Save to my plan. Do not say “Book now” when clicking only opens a guide. Do not claim a plan was saved or shared until the action succeeds.

## 8. Apparel and physical identity
Begin with three coherent core products: charcoal cap with paper embroidery, paper tee with charcoal mark, heavyweight charcoal tee with paper mark. Keep woven labels and interior details in the same neutral palette. Product names are descriptive, not fake collaborations.

Recommended starting art placements for sampling: cap front 55–65mm wide; tee left chest 70–90mm; centered chest 220–280mm; tote 180–220mm. These are sampling guides, not final factory tolerances. Verify the mark's narrow gaps at actual embroidery scale with a sew-out before bulk production.

Packaging: Paper White mailer or sleeve, charcoal wordmark, small charcoal seal. Neck label: standalone NSVL, size and separately verified care/origin information. Social templates: single photograph, restrained NSVL corner mark, one useful headline; distinguish editorial from product drops. Avoid printing the full website URL on every garment.

## 9. Recognition system
Consistency comes from the same mark, strong neutral palette, direct headlines and repeated cultural usefulness. Build recurring editions—weekly plans, neighborhood guides, original local stories, considered product releases. Apparel and editorial should share subjects and collaborators where rights are secured. Design alone does not establish global recognition or guarantee traffic.
